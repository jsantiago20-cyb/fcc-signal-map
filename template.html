How this was made

Source. Every coverage number comes from /nbm/map/api/mobile/detail/{process_uuid}/{lat}/{lon} — the same endpoint the FCC's own Location Summary sidebar calls. — points on a __SPACING__-mile hex lattice were queried against the — filing. Terrain is USGS 3DEP hillshade; roads, trails and rivers are OpenStreetMap via Overpass; county lines and town centroids are Census TIGERweb.

What a filled hex means. That a carrier told the FCC it expects service there — modeled propagation, not a measurement. The FCC's thresholds are the bar: 4G LTE at 5/1 Mbps, 5G-NR at either 7/1 or 35/3 Mbps, all outdoor and stationary. In-vehicle and in-building coverage are smaller.

Read the signal filter. Carriers also report the minimum signal strength behind each claim, and it ranges from −120 dBm to −50 dBm here. −120 dBm sits at or below the usable floor for most handsets. Set the filter to −100 dBm and watch how much claimed coverage disappears — that gap is the practical difference between the map and your phone.

Getting around. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to zoom in; the buttons top-left do the same and RST resets. Hover a hex for its full record, click to pin it. Paste any coordinate into the jump box — (38.246, -105.664), 38.246, -105.664, and 38.25228°N 105.66813°W all parse, as does degrees/minutes/seconds. Minor town labels appear as you zoom in.

Limits. Each hex is one point sample, not a polygon the FCC published, so edges are accurate to about __SPACING__ miles. Only the current filing answers this endpoint — older vintages return 403 — so no year-over-year comparison is possible.
